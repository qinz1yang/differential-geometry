# Actual scaled-cutoff derivatives

In particular FC05 is not a proof of the joint edge-cutoff network of FC10.

BilinearBounds proves first and second Frechet derivative estimates for actual compositions with an arbitrary continuous bilinear map. The second derivative has both product cross terms. SecondDerivativeComposition proves the actual nonlinear second-derivative chain rule bound and its constant linear-precomposition specialization, with the squared input norm in the Hessian estimate. These apply to arbitrary real normed spaces; no finite-dimensional or changing Poincare interfaces are used.

WeightedAffineCutoff proves derivative bounds for the actual function phi(x) times (Lx+b). Only on the topological support of phi must the affine weight have bounded norm. Outside that support, the product's first and second derivatives vanish. Consequently no bound on all ambient coordinates is introduced. This is the support argument needed for both FC05 and the later coordinate-strip application of FC10.

ScaledCutoffBlock is the actual map into the orthogonal L2 product, (phi(u/s) times u, s times phi(u/s)). Its C2 regularity and global first and second derivative bounds are proved: A=1+(C+1)L1 and B/s=(2L1+(C+1)L2)/s. The scale is any positive real number; there is no upper bound on it or on the point u. The source's closed-ball support hypothesis is transported through the actual constant scaling map. The Hessian is expressed as the derivative of the continuous-linear-map-valued Frechet derivative; its operator norm is the corresponding curried bilinear norm.

The final theorem binds these bounds to actual same-domain maps U,V, with pointwise value/derivative error epsilon and derivative bound L on V. It proves the maximum of the value and derivative errors after composition is at most (A+(B/s)L)epsilon. The pointwise statements quantify over all points and therefore supply the uniform version directly. The domain can be any real normed space and need not be convex. Binding actual Riemannian domain derivatives still belongs to the inherited interface; the orthogonal multiple-block estimate is supplied separately by BlockNormBounds. No global fibration or FC10 network is claimed from this layer.
