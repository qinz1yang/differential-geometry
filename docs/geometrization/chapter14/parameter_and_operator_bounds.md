A separate theorem proves well-foundedness from absence of directed cycles on a finite set, using the transitive closure rather than merely forbidding self-edges. MarkerRecovery proves FC03's original bound (1+A) epsilon/(1-epsilon) and a strictly positive marker, directly from distance in the actual L2 vector-plus-marker block. The argument applies to every original preimage and any normed real coordinate space. Domain membership follows in consumers from the actual cutoff's support condition, which is not assumed produced by this estimate.

BlockNormBounds proves the orthogonal square-sum operator estimate and the square-root-of-active-count bound. The total target dimension and number of inactive blocks do not enter the constant. DirectionalSaturation proves the common-unit-vector comparison first for Riesz vectors and then for actual continuous linear functionals. Their unit direction may be chosen independently at each point. Binding those tests to Riemannian derivatives and integrating on the original geodesic ball remain geometric consumers.

AdjointRankMargin proves surjectivity and the quantitative lower bound on the actual kernel complement from an adjoint lower bound, then proves its stability under operator-norm perturbation. Finite dimensions, the positive margin and the actual operators are explicit. A zero map cannot pass by having a trivial kernel complement. ProjectedGraphRank then proves the complete FC06 operator conclusion for the actual orthogonal projection onto the range of T: surjectivity, normal error at most e, and both kernel-complement bounds. Its assumption ||z|| <= ||Tz|| follows for the source graph Tz=(z,Gz) by projection to the first coordinate. The ambient target need not even be finite dimensional; only the source and graph parameter spaces need finite dimension. An actual smooth chart derivative or global submersion is not claimed constructed.

CompositionBounds proves the full derivative difference formula and its operator bound, then the pointwise max-of-value-and-derivative bound (A+B L) epsilon for actual Frechet derivatives. The second derivative bound is global in the target variable, so the joining segment may leave a cutoff support and the source domain need not be convex. Substituting B/s gives FC05's required improvement with a large zero-piece radius. The construction and support estimates of the particular cutoff network remain separate producers.
