`Analysis/Calculus/Compactness/FiniteOrder.lean` states the Euclidean metric-coefficient extraction in LFR14's Step2. A sequence of C^K symmetric bilinear-form fields on one open set has derivatives bounded on every compact subset only through order K. With one positive uniform ellipticity lower bound and a finite upper bound, a subsequence converges in the existing `MapCPConvergenceOn` predicate at order K−1 on each compact subset, to a C^{K−1} field retaining the same symmetry and ellipticity. No bound above K appears. The generic local statement allows K=1 (continuous limit) and the empty open set (all local assertions vacuous); this is the elementary finite-order Arzela–Ascoli part of the proof, not the full geometric theorem's K≥5 input. Source smoothness above K is unnecessary for this local extraction.

`Geometry/Metric/Pullback/FiniteRegularity.lean` uses the existing Mathlib `Bundle.ContMDiffRiemannianMetric`, not a new placeholder metric predicate. Given a C^K metric and an actual C^s diffeomorphism between supplied smooth carriers, every r with r≤K and r+1≤s admits the pullback metric of class C^r with the exact formula

    h.inner x v w = g.inner (f x) (mfderiv f x v) (mfderiv f x w).

This is the tensor itself, not a smooth approximation. The admissible-order inequalities are equivalent to the derivative guarantee r≤min(K,s−1), and enforce s≥1. The two real corollaries give C^m under a C^{m+1} map, and C^{m−4} under a C^{m−3} map (m≥4). In the latter, m=K−1 is precisely the zero-model K−5 loss in LFR47 and the final derivative audit. At m=4 only continuity is promised. The geometric consumer separately keeps its stronger K≥10, so its transferred metric is at least C^5.

The pullback theorem already assumes the compatible smooth carriers and actual finite diffeomorphism; it does not construct those carriers or maps. No claim concerning completeness, distance distortion, curvature passage, exceptional model classification, full-buffer coverage, or subsequent smooth fiber type follows merely from these signatures. Such conclusions remain separate interfaces/proofs.
