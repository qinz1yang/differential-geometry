## Mathematical statements and source boundary

- **LC01 analytic kernel:** `Real.firstPositiveLevel f w` is literally the infimum of positive radii where `f r = w`. Continuity on the positive half-line, limit `c` at zero from the right, limit zero at infinity, and `0 < w < c` prove positive attainment and `w < f r` at every earlier positive radius. The levels are strictly antitone in `w`. No monotonicity of `f` is assumed. The Riemannian instance requires the inherited volume continuity, small-ball asymptotics and finite-volume limit. This file proves the scalar argument; it does not claim those geometric bindings.
- **LC62:** an assigned positive radius with a finite upper bound has a maximal doubling-containing ball above every candidate whose ball meets an arbitrary set `Z`. The proof chooses a radius greater than half the supremum of reachable radii, and proves maximality; it does not assume the supremum is attained. The neighboring radius bound holds on the CLOSED ten-radius ball, including its outer sphere. There is no continuity or uniform positive-floor hypothesis for this step.
- **LC63:** total boundedness of the centers and a positive uniform radius floor make an abstract Vitali disjoint selection finite. Each original ball INTERSECTS a selected ball and has radius at most twice its radius. The three-radius center bound and OPEN five-radius inclusion are proved. No closedness of the center set or continuity of its radii is assumed.
- **LC64:** a positive continuous scale on a compact metric space bounds the ORIGINAL assigned radii between `T ρ` and `U ρ`. The preceding lemmas give a finite disjoint cover with the exact neighboring radius and scale-ratio estimates. A separate lemma converts the five-radius cover to an OPEN tenth-radius cover when the CLOSED shell, including the inner boundary, is excluded. Empty spaces and sets are allowed. All witnesses use the original radius function.
- **LC25:** a prescribed pointed Kleiner–Lott approximation to an actual `RadialConeData` supplies an outward point at exactly twice the original radius, with lower distance `r` and strict upper distance `r + 15 δ`. The proof uses actual coverage witnesses (error strictly less than `2 δ`) and trims an actual minimizing segment. The general theorem needs `δ < r` and `2r + 5δ < δ⁻¹`; the annulus corollary derives them from the exact Blueprint bounds. No curvature, differentiability, completeness, or nontrivial cone is silently assumed.

## Fixed scales and low-dimensional models

- **LC22:** `PointedGHConverges.rescale` preserves convergence for every fixed positive multiplier, using the actual rescaled metric structures and the existing completeness equivalence. `eventually_approx_fixed_target` composes the actual bounded-domain maps with a prescribed map into one fixed target, at the exact threshold `min (δ/100) (1/(2*(2*(δ⁻¹+δ)+4)))`. The rescaled corollary chooses `R` before the tail.
- **LC23 metric argument:** `exists_uniform_bounded_rescaling` works with arbitrary families of pointed metric spaces and a fixed family of model/target pairs. Subsequence compactness and convergence of every model at all sufficiently large real rescalings produce a bounded interval containing one successful scale, uniformly over all parameters on a tail. The index family is a sequence indexed by natural numbers; no claim about an independently real-indexed family is made. The theorem applies to the source's cone targets, and proves a stronger general metric statement without requiring cone structure in the compactness argument. Tits-cone existence is still an input.
- **LC17–18:** common-limit extraction from complete nonnegative models of dimension at most `n` preserves the same limit for arbitrary sources approximating them. For `1 ≤ n < k`, a single positive tolerance excludes simultaneous approximation to such a model and a normalized `k`-splitting. Both errors may vary independently below that tolerance. The transverse factor remains arbitrary; its completeness, properness and dimension are not assumed. The contradiction uses the actual onto product limit and its Euclidean slice, not a product-dimension equality. The source case is `n=2`, `k=3`.
