Its quality is any positive prescribed number; in particular it supplies LC11's `1/320000`. `PairedPacketAnchors` proves that sufficiently good packets have all six anchors distinct and, with the existing exclusion of the center, gives the source's distinctness requirements. It also produces positive lower and finite upper anchor-distance bounds from the actual finite packet.

`UniformPacketPerturbation` chooses one side-length tolerance and one positive radius before the approximating metric space and its points. The original and lifted center-to-anchor and anchor-to-anchor errors imply the requested packet quality throughout that entire radius-two buffer. Positive lower and finite upper central-distance bounds hold throughout the same buffer. The proof uses the existing uniform comparison-angle continuity theorem on an explicit compact side window. It does not invoke a separate qualitative neighborhood for each approximating space.

`UniformLiftedPacket` uses actual fixed-radius pointed approximations to lift the packet on the original sequence's full late tail. It returns one common radius and comparison region, all anchors in that region, center distance below 1/2, the whole radius-two buffer in that region, uniform distance bounds and a complete closed ball. Its primary source assumptions are complete source metrics and the eventual ambient-distance four-point comparison on each fixed ball. The Riemannian mechanism producing those comparison regions is not hidden in this theorem. No new subsequence is taken.

`UniformImageCube` specializes the proved residual-correction engine to three distance coordinates. With the displayed source constants it proves `mu >= 9/10`, positive `e`, the entire closed coordinate cube of half-side `e/4` in the image of the OPEN source ball of radius `r/2`, and the Euclidean Lipschitz bound `sqrt 3`. It retains the precise cutoff minimum and strict displacement bound, including a zero initial residual. It requires neither an injective coordinate map nor a different intrinsic metric.

`NormalizedHausdorffMeasure` defines the positive normalization factor using the actual Haar scalar factor between Euclidean Lebesgue measure and mathlib's Hausdorff measure. It proves equality to Euclidean volume, preservation of the Lipschitz image estimate under this normalization, and the volume formula for an actual coordinate cube. This avoids identifying mathlib's raw Hausdorff measure with Euclidean volume: mathlib's `hausdorffMeasure_pi_real` normalizes the sup-norm function space, while this chapter uses the Euclidean norm. The official mathlib sources actually read were `MeasureTheory/Measure/Hausdorff.lean:730–747,888–1000`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/Unique.lean:265–287,415–427`, `MeasureTheory/Measure/Haar/InnerProductSpace.lean:132–140`, and `MeasureTheory/Measure/Lebesgue/Basic.lean:235–242`, all at the pinned mathlib revision above.

`CubeMeasureBound` proves the general cube/image lower bound and the exact three-dimensional specialization `(e/2)^3 / (3*sqrt 3) > 0`. It applies to an actual Lipschitz map on a set and uses outer-measure inequalities; no measurable-image, injectivity or differentiability assumption is added.

`LimitVolumeLowerBound` assembles these producers. Under the actual pointed convergence, source complete metrics and short curves, eventual ambient comparison, and the limit's nonnegative comparison and dimension between two and three, it derives the limit short curves from the source curves and convergence and gives one positive normalized Hausdorff-measure lower bound for the radius-two ball on the WHOLE late tail.
