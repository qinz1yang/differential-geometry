# AC50: actual approximate factors have one complete proper limit

Three public theorems in two leaves prove the full metric AC50 contract.
AC49's normalized input is represented by the EXISTING KleinerLottApprox
with actual target WithLp2(EuclideanSpace R(Fin k) x Z_i), basepoint(0,b_i)
and its supplied whole-space map. No parallel approximation predicate is
introduced. The results are stronger: the left metric factors E_i may vary,
with any specified basepoints a_i. Only the actual L2 product metric matters.

Assume (X_i,p_i) converges to the given proper metric target(Y,q), supplied
whole-space KleinerLottApprox maps X_i -> E_i x_2 Z_i have errors delta_i,
and delta_i tends to zero. Each map's structure already includes0<delta_i<1,
pointedness, distortion on the open reciprocal-radius ball and INFIMUM
coverage on the smaller open target ball. No source or factor completeness,
properness, curvature, dimension, length structure or map continuity is
assumed. The target is complete through the convergence contract/properness.

For EVERY R,eta>0, a single finite N and tail I are produced so that every
factor closed R-ball on that tail has an INTERNAL eta-net of size at most N.
The bound and tail are independent of the point in the ball and member of
the tail. One subsequence then converges to ONE complete proper factor
space. Convergence to the ORIGINAL source target is retained along that
same subsequence. Independent limits of separate balls are not used.

The proof uses an explicit alternative to AC50's assignment-class argument.
Choose epsilon=min(1/100,eta/100), one finite eta/4-net in the compact original
limit ball of radiusR+1, and reverse source approximations on R+4 balls.
Convert the supplied product maps to strict closed-ball approximations with
slack, enlarge error to epsilon, and compose on R+2 balls with error4epsilon.
The accepted net-transfer theorem gives an internal product R-ball net;
projection of its centers gives an internal factor net with no cardinality
increase. All reciprocal-radius, domain and error margins are proved. The
source proof's nonattained-infimum issue is handled by the accepted2delta
witness/3delta closed-ball conversion, never by claiming an exact nearest point.
