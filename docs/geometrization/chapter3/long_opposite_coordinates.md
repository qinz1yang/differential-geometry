# Full AC61: original endpoint lines and their individual coordinates

Three public theorems in two leaves close AC61 with the actual oriented prefix
witnesses retained. The generic theorem starts with the original specified
proper pointed limit, global nonnegative comparison and geodesic segments in
that target, and short curves in the source metric spaces. It accepts any finite
family of endpoint pairs with positive (possibly pair-dependent) arm lengths
tending to infinity, actual absolute excess tending to zero, and any positive
short-curve error tending to zero. Source completeness is not required there.

It returns ONE strict subsequence, convergence to the SAME original target and
basepoint, growing radii, vanishing errors, and actual closed-ball approximation
maps. It ALSO returns the actual signed prefixes, their 1-Lipschitz and
basepoint properties, all four signed distance calibrations, and simultaneous
uniform convergence of these prefixes on every bounded signed interval to the
returned isometric lines. The exact same maps and lines satisfy, simultaneously
for every member and every point of any fixed source ball,

    |d(o_i,aPlus_i,j)-d(x,aPlus_i,j)-lineCoordinate(gamma_j)(f_i(x))| -> 0.

Every line passes through the original basepoint. The positive-ray Busemann
limit is returned explicitly as -lineCoordinate(gamma_j), hence the expression
above is the blueprint's h_i,j + b_j(f_i(x)). The positive endpoint fixes its
orientation. Canonical onto line splitting is constructed separately for each
line; no splitting or coordinate convergence is assumed as an input.

The second theorem PRODUCES the excess limit using full AC57 from opposite
comparison angles in curvature -sigma_i, with original arm lengths sigma_i
inverse and curve error sigma_i. It only assumes sigma_i>0 and sigma_i->0; the
angle condition may itself be eventual. Early sigma_i>1 do not require deleting
or changing original indices. The third theorem derives the target comparison
and geodesic facts from the original complete source spaces, short curves,
local comparison in the open reciprocal balls, their Hausdorff dimension bound,
and the specified proper pointed convergence. It uses the accepted 256R
interior-buffer route only on fixed late balls. No comparison is applied to the
far endpoints or their entire long paths. Properness of the specified target
is retained as in AC61's AC41-produced target.

Finite indexing may be empty. The common source-radius inclusion is proved
independently of choosing an index. Opposite conditions alone permit repeated
or nonorthogonal endpoint pairs. No distinctness, cross-pair orthogonality or
common higher-rank splitting is asserted. AC53/62-66 supply those separate
steps; Chapters3-4 remain unfinished and migration interfaces remain paused.
