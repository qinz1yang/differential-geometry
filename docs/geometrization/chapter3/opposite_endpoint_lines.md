# AC59: actual calibrated prefixes and lines in the supplied approximation target

Four public theorems in four leaves connect original short-curve/endpoints data
to actual lines in the SAME prescribed proper target. No varying source space
is assumed complete, proper, geodesic or Alexandrov.

The first theorem joins the actual positive and negative prefixes at their common
basepoint, retaining exact orientation. It constructs Q:[-L,L]->X, proves it is
1-Lipschitz, that Q(0)=o, and that its restriction at plus/minus t is the supplied
prefix. Its lower distance bound is |s-t|-E-2eta; radial distances are <=|t|.
The second theorem constructs this signed curve directly from original short
curves and equal-distance opposite endpoints. E is the actual absolute excess
2L-d(aPlus,aMinus). All four exact AC58 signed calibration inequalities survive.

The third theorem accepts actual PointedBallApprox maps from the varying sources
to the specified Y, with radii tending to infinity and errors tending to zero,
and actual signed 1-Lipschitz prefixes. Both lengths L(i,j) and distance deficits
delta(i,j) may vary with j in ANY finite indexing type. Each length tends to
infinity and each absolute deficit tends to zero. A finite-product norm gives
a common vanishing bound; no uniformity over an infinite family is asserted.
The actual prefix images yield ONE family of isometric lines in Y and ONE strict
subsequence, with uniform convergence over all family members and every bounded
signed parameter interval. Source membership in each approximation domain is
proved by radial control. Arbitrary behavior outside the available domains is
used only to totalize intermediate maps and disappears on every eventual fixed
parameter interval. Coverage and continuity of the approximation maps are unused.

The fourth theorem produces the actual signed prefixes from original finite
length-space inputs, paired endpoints at positive distance L(i,j), absolute
excess tending to zero and any positive eta(i) tending to zero. It returns those
actual prefixes, their Lipschitz/basepoint properties and all signed calibration
bounds, together with the isometric lines and one strict subsequence. The SAME
supplied target, approximation maps, endpoint orientation and source indices are
retained. It does not assume the endpoint pairs themselves lie inside the finite
approximation domains. Positive/negative real parameter convergence is one whole
line, not unrelated limits of separate balls or rays.
