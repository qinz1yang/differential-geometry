## Consult the relevant references before fixing mathematical contracts

The local reference archive is `/Users/bennettchow/Documents/Codex/Geometrization/BooksPapers`. Treat it as read-only. Use the existing reference inventory, source manifest, and comparison records to locate relevant material.

Before introducing or substantially revising a mathematical theorem contract, proof route, dependency claim, or reuse/gap classification:

1. Read the relevant source theorem, its definitions, and the proof passages needed to establish the proposed dependencies. Expand the reading when an imported result or hidden hypothesis matters.
2. Check the full hypotheses, quantifier order and uniformity, regularity, curvature and time normalization, metric and domain conventions, boundary conditions, and precise conclusion. Distinguish local results from their applications to flow and from final reconstruction.
3. Record the actual source version, theorem/section identifier, printed page, and PDF page when applicable. For TeX sources, record the label or an exact section/line locator. Verify the body rather than relying on a filename or contents page. Tie records to existing source hashes where available.
4. Check applicable author or publisher errata. Use authoritative external sources when the local copy is incomplete, ambiguous, or lacks corrections; distinguish externally checked material from the archived version.
5. Compare sources when their statements, conventions, or graph/geometric definitions differ. Name the required translations and leave unresolved implications explicit. Do not silently strengthen a conclusion, weaken a hypothesis, or treat a citation as an adapter proof.
6. Update the relevant existing inventories and comparison records with the contract. If evidence exposes a mismatch in the current design, record it and assess the affected consumers before changing the contract.

Use targeted reading. Access to the collection does not mean every work has been read. Reuse documented source checks when the source and relevant claim are unchanged; reopen passages when a new question or discrepancy warrants it. Do not restart completed work merely to impose this policy retrospectively.
