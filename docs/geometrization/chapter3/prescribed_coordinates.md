# Full AC52: prescribed coordinates on the whole source space

Three public theorems and four definitions in three leaves prove AC52's finite
error and eventual whole-space conclusions. The left factor may be any metric
space, so the Euclidean norm statement is a direct specialization.

A target isometry transports an actual pointed approximation without changing
its radius/error. Perturbing its actual map by distance<=rho at every controlled
point, while preserving the basepoint, gives error epsilon+2rho on the SAME
closed radius. Nonnegativity ofrho follows from the bound at the basepoint; it
is not an additional hypothesis. The proof checks both distortion inequalities
and coverage, includingrho=0. Replacing only the first product coordinate keeps
the transverse coordinate exactly and moves each point by precisely its first
coordinate discrepancy in the actual L2 metric. Its first coordinate is exactly
the prescribed whole-space functionh on the controlled ball.

The whole-space KL constructor uses a closed approximation of radius at least
1/delta with error<delta/2. Inside that closed domain it uses the actual map;
outside it uses(h(x),b). The coverage witness is proved to lie in the required
OPEN reciprocal-radius source ball using radial distortion and error slack.
The KL distortion and infimum coverage bounds are<=delta. The separate first
coordinate theorem proves equality toh EVERYWHERE, provided it holds on the
controlled domain. No continuity/smoothness/submersion property is assumed.

For actual source approximations on growing domains with errors tending tozero,
a fixed onto product isometry and prescribed pointed functionsh_i whose coordinate
discrepancy tends uniformly tozero on every fixed ball, the final theorem gives:
for EACH0<delta<1, ALL sufficiently late source members have actual KL maps whose
first coordinate equalsh_i on the entire source space. No subsequence is introduced
by this restoration step. The proof restricts toS=1/delta+delta, takes original
error<delta/32 and discrepancy<=delta/16, applies the finite perturbation estimate,
then the whole-space construction. No properness, completeness, length, curvature,
dimension or approximation-map continuity is required for this metric theorem.
