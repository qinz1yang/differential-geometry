# Buffered radial comparison and fixed-radius orthogonality

Four public theorems in three leaves join the completed coarse local-comparison
route to AC63, and provide the final target-side two-sign orthogonality step.
They are components of AC65/ALG08, not yet its original-input assembly.

The buffered comparison theorem assumes an actual complete finite metric space,
arbitrarily short continuous curves, kappa>=0, r>0, 256r<L, ambient Hausdorff
dimension<=n on the ORIGINAL OPEN ball B(p,L), and local four-point comparison
of curvature -kappa there. Two SUPPLIED isometric radial segments may have
unequal lengths A,B>=r. For every0<s,t<=r the actual comparison angle at s,t
is at least its value at r,r, using ambient distances throughout. The accepted
256r local-input producer supplies comparison on the open2r ball. The proof
retains those same supplied segments, uses their exact distance equations,
and applies accepted radial-angle monotonicity twice. No complete/proper
endpoint ball or globally proper source is assumed. Endpoint times are allowed;
zero time is excluded. Kappa=0 is included. This does NOT prove the old AC64
sharp8r margin.

The second source theorem combines that result with actual AC63 shortening.
Its two supplied radial isometries have common length L. From their original
endpoint comparison angle>=theta>0, kappa>0 and the same256r<L geometry, it
returns AC63's explicit clipped lower bound at EVERY fixed0<T<=r on these
EXACT paths. Radial/tail distances are proved from isometry; original endpoints
are not replaced. Choosing r=L/1024 in a later sequence consumer makes the
buffer strict and the lower bound tends to pi/2 for the original long-strainer
scale. This theorem alone does not extract a limiting family.

The target-side theorem needs only global nonnegative four-point comparison,
two actual unit-speed lines with the same origin, and at ONE fixed T>0 the two
comparison angles (gamma(T),beta(T)) and (gamma(T),beta(-T)) both>=pi/2.
It proves their canonical positive-ray germ angle is exactly pi/2. The proof
uses accepted crossing-line coordinate identities: the two inequalities force
the crossing coefficient and its negative to be nonpositive. A companion gives
Pythagoras for ALL signed time parameters. No properness, supplied geodesics,
finite-dimensionality or product-splitting theorem is required by these target
lemmas. Their use of global target comparison is explicit.
