# Distance-map openness from a uniform paired packet

Four new leaves assemble AC21–AC23 on actual finite distance-coordinate
maps. `PairedComparisonPacket` records precisely the opposite and all four
cross-pair comparison-angle inequalities at points of a supplied working
set. It is an explicit proposition about the existing model angles, not
an opaque curvature, strainer or tangent-space predicate. Anchor bounds,
the common comparison domain, short curves and completeness stay separate.

Restriction, tolerance weakening and swapping the two anchors are proved.
The directed coordinate-move theorem keeps the original first-anchor
coordinate family even when moving toward a second anchor. This matters:
swapping the entire distance map would not prove the required residual step.

The finite residual theorem constructs a point in the same working set,
with the blueprint mu=1-3*delta^2-6*(m-1)*delta and contraction 1-mu/m.
It proves e/m<=step<=e, the actual l1 residual contraction and the sharp
displacement budget. Coordinate residuals are bounded individually by
the geometric scale; an unnecessary bound on their sum is not imposed
in that one-step theorem. Nonempty finite index types retain m>=1.

The complete-buffer consumer assumes a complete closed r-ball at q and
an OPEN 2r-ball contained in the working set. For targets with initial
residual at most every geometric scale and at most mu*r/2, it produces
an exact preimage in the SAME closed r/2-ball, with distance at most e0/mu.
Its proof uses the already audited residual-sublevel invariant. Geometric
moves are now proved from the packet; they are no longer separate assumptions
of this preimage theorem. Zero residuals and the allowed buffer boundary
are included.

The quantitative image theorem proves positivity of the exact target radius

    epsilon=min(1,a0/2,delta^2/K,mu*r/2)

and B(F(q),epsilon) subset F(B(q,r)). Here K remains exactly
4*cosh(A+1)/sinh(a0). The open-map theorem repeats this construction inside
EVERY open subset of the open working region. Its local-completeness input
is explicit: each point has some complete closed positive-radius ambient
ball. Smaller complete balls follow by the previously proved buffer lemma.
The inherited metric on the working region is not assumed to be a length
metric. All short curves are in the original space.

Openness is also transported to every PiLp target with its canonical
product topology, including the Euclidean p=2 target. The general p
open-map theorem is topological and does not assert a normed-space or
Lipschitz structure when p<1. Norm bounds and Hausdorff consumers continue
to require p>=1 as proved in the preceding distance-coordinate checkpoint.
