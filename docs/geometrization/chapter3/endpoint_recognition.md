# AC47: the global endpoint branch

Eight public theorems in five leaves globalize AC46's endpoint case.
In any space with actual minimizing segments, a positive closed ball lies
in the closure of its open ball: points on the boundary are approached
along actual radial segments. Applying this to the accepted endpoint
no-exit theorem proves that the entire endpoint-centered closed ball,
up to the length of a supplied segment, is exactly that segment's range.
Compactness of the segment gives closedness; the space itself need not
be complete or proper at this stage.

If all minimizing segment interiors are open and p is a metric endpoint
(dist(x,p)+dist(p,y)=dist(x,y) implies x=p or y=p), then x -> dist(p,x)
is a GLOBAL isometry into the real line. Comparing arbitrary points inside
the segment to the farther one proves the exact distance identity, with
zero-distance cases treated separately.

A complete nontrivial space with this isometric distance coordinate and
continuous curves from p has image either [0,L], L>0, or [0,infinity).
Completeness makes the image closed. Curves fill every intermediate
coordinate; boundedness supplies the attained supremum, while unboundedness
fills the entire ray. The constructed isometry satisfies e(x)=dist(p,x)
for EVERY x. Thus another basepoint retains its actual coordinate; it is
not silently moved to a boundary endpoint.

Two metric adapters prove that injectivity of distance from p on a positive
ball makes p a metric endpoint, and that a pointed half-interval chart
supplies exactly that injectivity. Consequently, for a complete nontrivial
geodesic space with global fourPointComparison 0 and dimH<=1, either an
explicit metric endpoint or an actual AC46 pointed half-interval chart
produces the global interval/ray model. Segment-interior openness is derived
by the accepted AC46 proof, not added as a geometric hypothesis.
