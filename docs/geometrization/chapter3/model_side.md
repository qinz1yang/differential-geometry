# Actual model sides and the infinite cradle limit

Nineteen public theorems, one model-side definition and two private identities
in four leaves provide the actual cosine-law side function for curvature
-kappa, kappa>=0. At zero curvature it is the square root of the Euclidean
cosine-law expression; at negative curvature it is arcosh of the hyperbolic
cosine-law expression divided by sqrt(kappa). Public results concern this
actual real side length, not a substitute proposition or assumed side witness.

The proofs give nonnegativity, symmetry, zero-arm values, angle-zero and
straight-angle values, monotonicity on [0,pi], and the full triangle side
window [abs(a-b),a+b]. For positive arms, comparison angle and model side
are inverse in both directions, including collinear triangles and opposite
side zero when the arms agree. Continuity holds at nonnegative arm lengths,
including zero arms; curvature is fixed. Uniform continuity on compact arm
windows then proves that when angles tend to pi, the model-side deficit
(a+b)-S tends to zero even if the arms themselves do not converge.

The model extension identity equates the old-center model side with the
new-center model side for the complementary comparison angle after extending
one side. Its proof uses the exact squared/cosh cosine laws and injectivity
on nonnegative lengths. It includes collinear triangles; the three short
triangle sides a,h,c are positive, and h<b. A consumer turns the two stated
short/adjacent comparison inequalities into nonincrease of the actual model
side. No unproved model-point realization or adjacent-angle equality is used.

For the accepted scalar cradle recurrence, comparison angles tend to pi.
Any actual angle sequence in [0,pi] dominating those short comparison angles
therefore tends to pi, and the corresponding model-side deficit tends to
zero. If those actual model sides are also antitone and every arm sum is
at least D, then EVERY model side is at least D. The antitone premise is
explicit; constructing geometric centers/joins and deriving their angle
inequalities and step-by-step nonincrease still requires assembly. These
results do not by themselves prove ALG02 or globalization.
