# AC13: the actual induced metric is a length metric

Four public theorems in two leaves complete AC13's remaining induced-length
claim. For a continuous curve c on the unit interval, every subinterval
bounds the intrinsic distance between its endpoints by its original
variation. Monotone clamping supplies an actual path with those endpoints.
Summing over arbitrary partitions and using exact variation additivity proves
that the curve's variation in the constructed intrinsic extended metric
is exactly its original variation. This includes infinite variation. It is
an equality for the same function, not a claim of continuity in the new
topology for every extended metric space. The finite metric conversion has
the same equality.

On an open ambient ball in a length space, the preceding topology theorem
DOES identify the intrinsic and subspace topologies. The all-path infimum
and proved finiteness therefore provide, for every pair and every positive
real epsilon, a continuous curve of intrinsic length less than the intrinsic
distance plus epsilon. The constructed metric's topology, weak extended
metric and real distance parents are explicit throughout the public result.
No compactness, completeness, minimizing segment, or assumed intrinsic
length property is used. The epsilon criterion is also valid for equal
endpoints and for endpoints arbitrarily near the open boundary.

Combined with the accepted IntrinsicBall, IntrinsicBallTopology and
IntrinsicBallCompleteness leaves, this proves AC13: finite actual intrinsic
distance, induced length property, equality with the subspace topology,
exact local distances on the stated4h-buffered closed balls, equality of
those closed balls under inclusion, and their completeness when the ambient
space is complete. The construction uses curves in the original topology;
topology identification is proved before their continuity is transferred.
